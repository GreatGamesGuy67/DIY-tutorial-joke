DIY Real-Life Biometric HUD Glasses

PROJECT LOG: Sensation indicators mapped to a retro graphical interface. This hardware relies on highly speculative data interpretation from external medical sensor patches. Do not mistake a blood sugar crash from skipped lunch for an actual in-game phantom attack.

Required Inventory

  • Chassis Matrix: 1x Pair of plastic lab safety goggles or old glasses
  • Optical Engine: 2x Micro transparent OLED display modules or small prisms
  • Logic Control: 1x Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W (Compact HUD driver)
  • Sensor Node: 4x Silver-chloride medical electrode patches with snap cables
  • Biometric Interface: 1x Pulse oximeter & muscle sensor breakout board
  • Power Plant: 1x Flat lithium-polymer battery pack (secured to head strap)
  • Adhesive Bond: 1x Roll of electrical tape & thin copper wire
  • Structural Core: 1x Tube of superglue & clear plastic strips

Assembly Guide

Step 1: The Optical Prism Rig

Take your micro transparent displays and mount them to the inner upper rim of the safety goggles using superglue. Angle them slightly downward so the graphical data projects directly into your field of view without totally blocking your ability to see actual walls or furniture.

Step 2: Wiring the Bio-Sensor Array

Run the snap cables from your medical electrode patches along the frame of the glasses, securing them tightly with electrical tape. Leave enough slack so the cables can reach your neck and temple areas comfortably without pulling your ears back.

Step 3: Calibrating the Heart Matrix

Affix two electrode patches to your temples to measure skin conductance and pulse rate. Connect these inputs to your sensor breakout board. Write a Python script on the Raspberry Pi that translates a steady heartbeat into ten beautifully rendered red pixel hearts on your screen. If your pulse spikes because you ran up the stairs, the script drops you down to four hearts and sounds an alarm.

Step 4: The Hunger Algorithm Engine

Place the remaining two electrodes along your jawline or upper neck. The sensor board tracks tiny micro-voltages from muscle contractions (like swallowing or stomach-rumbling vibrations). The software correlates hours of zero jaw movement with a dropping food bar, slowly deleting the hunger shanks from your display until you chew something.

Step 5: Wiring the Intrusive Damage Feedback Loop

Program an interrupt function into your core script. If an electrode snaps off or registers an unexpected, chaotic spike in connection quality, the Raspberry Pi fires a full RGB (255, 0, 0) crimson display pulse directly into your retinas. This serves as an incredibly annoying, highly disruptive visual flashbang to mimic video game combat damage.

Step 6: System Initialization

Stick the patches to your skin, flip the battery toggle, and watch your vision populate with full-color health and food bars. Enjoy walking around your house experiencing the world like a video game character, trying to figure out exactly how many hunger points a slice of cold pizza will restore in real time.
